## Goal

Three documents exist: `docs/CONTRIBUTING.md`, `docs/STYLE-RUBRIC.md`, and
`docs/AUTHORING-WITH-AI.md`. After this task, an outside contributor with no prior
context can read them, author a tree by hand or with AI assistance, run `lst validate`,
`lst ids`, and `lst lint` locally, and open a PR with a reasonable expectation of passing
CI and clearing two review rounds — S2 measured directly, and the thing F40's "know your
tree is correct before you open a PR" promise is actually testing.

## Why this shape

**Architecturally, `docs/AUTHORING-WITH-AI.md` is documentation, not software** — §6.7 is
explicit that this section of the pipeline "lives in `docs/AUTHORING-WITH-AI.md` and
ships no code, no service, and no CI integration." NG12 already bars AI from the running
app; keeping AI assistance out of the pipeline too means "every published tree is
human-reviewed" (F42) has no automated back door. That is why this task's deliverables
are three markdown files and nothing under `tools/` or `app/` — a prompt template that
ships as a script or a service would be a different, larger, and explicitly rejected
task.

**F43's Mozilla rule is load-bearing text, not a paraphrase target.** R-03 — semantic
redefinition under a stable uid — has no mechanical mitigation anywhere in the pipeline;
the *only* thing standing between an author quietly changing a milestone's meaning while
keeping its uid and a user's stale completion silently surviving is a reviewer who has
internalized the rule. That is why §5.4 says the rubric must carry it "verbatim," and why
this task's acceptance criteria check for the literal wording rather than "a rule to this
effect."

**D-15's professionalization finding is the reason a style rubric exists at all rather
than being folded into CONTRIBUTING.md as a paragraph.** `docs/PRIOR-ART.md` §7.3
documents MakerSkillTree's own contributors reproducing the teach/sell/publish-at-the-top
error across tree after tree, and the fix the architecture adopted (D-15) is explicitly
**editorial, not a lint gate** — "a regex on teach/sell/publish would false-positive on
legitimately advanced milestones such as 'teach a certification course.'" A rubric a
human reads and applies with judgment is the actual mechanism; T22's `lst lint` only
advises around the edges.

## Scope

**In scope**

- `docs/CONTRIBUTING.md`: the author-facing extract of §5 (Content Schema) and §6
  (Content Pipeline) — how to write a tree YAML file field by field, the full `lst`
  command table and when to run each subcommand, the two-round human review process and
  where to check status (`content/REVIEW-STATUS.md`, generated by T22's `lst status`),
  and a pointer to the exemplar trees (T05's cooking, T21's piano and mental-health) as
  worked references for the three progression shapes.
- `docs/STYLE-RUBRIC.md` (F43): milestone phrasing standards (concrete and testable, F2;
  no effort-quantity phrasing, matching T22's `vague-milestone` lint's own targets so the
  rubric and the lint agree on what they are each catching), level pacing and tier
  calibration guidance, requirement-group usage guidance (when to reach for `all` versus
  `n_of` versus `any`, including F46's warning that `all` groups at or below the blocker
  level should be reserved for content genuinely presupposed by everything above it), the
  professionalization-is-not-mastery rule with its facet-tag mechanism (F19, F28), and
  the Mozilla identity rule (F43, R-03) carried verbatim.
- `docs/AUTHORING-WITH-AI.md` (D15, §6.7): the four-step documented workflow — gather an
  existing curriculum or framework and check F45's copyleft carve-out first, draft
  against a versioned prompt template, normalize by hand, validate with `lst validate`
  and `lst lint` — plus the versioned prompt template itself (carrying the house rules
  inline: the 1–10 spine, 4–8 milestones per level, observable-completion-condition
  phrasing, no effort quantities, the professionalization rule), and an explicit,
  prominent statement of what is **not** built: no roadmap-ingestion tool, no AI service,
  no generation endpoint, no bot that opens PRs.
- Cross-references between all three documents and to `docs/PRD.md` / `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`
  §5–§6 where a contributor would reasonably want the underlying rationale rather than
  just the rule.

**Out of scope**

- The `lst` CLI itself and its seven lint rules — T03, T22, already built. This task
  documents their use; it does not implement them.
- The exemplar trees the rubric and CONTRIBUTING.md point to as worked examples — T05,
  T21, both already blocking dependencies of this task specifically so they exist as
  citable references.
- `content/REVIEW-STATUS.md`'s generation — T22's `lst status`. This task only tells a
  contributor where to look.
- Any AI service, ingestion tool, bot, or CI integration for AI-assisted authoring — D15
  and §6.7 rule this out explicitly, and this task's job is to document that it does not
  exist, not to build a smaller version of it.
- Promoting any `lst lint` rule to a hard gate — R-04, a future maintainer decision made
  on evidence, not something this task's rubric should presuppose or argue for.
- The CI workflow that runs these checks and gates PRs — T25.
- Domain/subregion/facet taxonomy content itself — T02, T12. This task documents how to
  use the taxonomy, not what it contains.

## Interface contract

The identity rule `docs/STYLE-RUBRIC.md` must carry verbatim, copied from the PRD's F43
(*Strengthened in v1.3*):

> The rubric shall also state when a revised milestone remains **the same milestone**,
> adopting Mozilla's localization rule verbatim: *a typo or clarity fix keeps the
> milestone's identity; a change of meaning requires a new one.*

And the architecture's own statement of the same rule, from §5.4, which the rubric should
also carry given it is stated there as the literal mitigation for **R-03**:

> *a typo or clarity fix keeps the uid; a change of meaning requires a new uid and a
> lineage entry.*

The professionalization rule `docs/STYLE-RUBRIC.md` must state, copied verbatim from F43:

> The rubric shall state that **professionalization is not mastery**: teaching, selling,
> publishing, competing, and certifying are modes of engagement available at many
> levels, not markers of the top tier, and shall not be placed at levels 9–10 by default.
> A beginner can teach a beginner. These are expressed as **facet tags** (F19), orthogonal
> to level, and the initial controlled vocabulary shall reserve terms for them.

The four-step AI-assisted authoring workflow `docs/AUTHORING-WITH-AI.md` must document,
copied verbatim from §6.7:

> 1. **Gather** an existing curriculum or graded framework for the skill (F44) — ABRSM
>    syllabus, CEFR descriptors, a belt curriculum, a published course outline — plus the
>    author's own expertise. F45's copyleft carve-out is checked *here*, before any
>    drafting, because that is the last cheap moment to check it.
> 2. **Draft** against a published prompt template that carries the house rules inline:
>    the 1–10 spine, 4–8 milestones per level, achievement phrasing with observable
>    completion conditions, no effort quantities, and the professionalization-is-not-
>    mastery rule from F43.
> 3. **Normalize** by hand. The author rewrites every milestone in their own words and
>    deletes anything they cannot personally judge. This step is the one the guide should
>    press hardest on, because an unedited draft is exactly the "empty container with an
>    AI-generated veneer" the PRD's §3 trade-offs name as the failure mode to avoid.
> 4. **Validate** with `lst validate` and `lst lint`, then open the PR.

The load-bearing exclusion list `docs/AUTHORING-WITH-AI.md` must state, copied verbatim
from §6.7:

> **Explicitly not built:** no roadmap-ingestion tool, no AI service, no generation
> endpoint, no bot that opens PRs. Each would create an ops surface (N10) and would put
> unreviewed content one merge-button away from publication.

The copyleft carve-out `docs/CONTRIBUTING.md` and `docs/AUTHORING-WITH-AI.md` must both
surface, copied verbatim from F45:

> F44 covers *structure and sequencing* drawn from published frameworks, cited and not
> reproduced. It shall not be read as permission to adapt content under a **ShareAlike
> licence** (CC BY-SA, CC BY-NC-SA)... The contribution checklist shall ask explicitly
> whether any part of a submission derives from a copyleft-licensed source, and
> CI-passing trees with an affirmative answer shall be rejected at review.

## Notes and hazards

- **Both worked examples now exist, and one of them has a caveat — F29.** T21 landed
  `content/trees/piano.yaml` (three tracks, 15 cross-track edges, explicit multi-group
  `requirements:` at levels 4 and 7) and `content/trees/mental-health.yaml` (five modules,
  `n_of`/`any` electives at levels 4, 6 and 10). Point the rubric at both. But **do not
  describe module labels as something the reader will see on screen**: §9 draws neither a
  track title nor a module label, so as of 2026-08-15 the modular tree renders exactly like
  a linear one. See F29 in `docs/SPEC-FINDINGS.md`. Modules are real in the data, and
  worth authoring, and currently invisible — say that rather than implying a picture the
  contributor will not get.
- **~~T20 also writes into `docs/CONTRIBUTING.md`~~ — resolved 2026-08-14. The collision is
  gone.** T20 took the other of its two options and put the record in the §16.2 checklist
  location, which it created as **`docs/RELEASE-CHECKLIST.md`**, precisely because this task
  authors CONTRIBUTING.md wholesale and the two are unordered. This task must therefore
  **not** write a walkthrough record, a release checklist, or an accessibility verification
  table into CONTRIBUTING.md — link to `docs/RELEASE-CHECKLIST.md` instead. A second copy
  would be a dated verification artifact with two dates, which is worse than none.
- **R-03 has no mechanical backstop.** This task's rubric is, per §5.4 and §19.3, the
  *entire* mitigation for semantic redefinition under a stable uid, alongside two-round
  review (F42). Do not treat the Mozilla-rule paragraph as boilerplate to satisfy a
  checklist — it is the one sentence in the whole pipeline standing between an edit and a
  silent data-integrity failure nothing else catches.
- **Do not let `docs/AUTHORING-WITH-AI.md` grow into a spec for a tool.** The temptation,
  writing a "how to use AI to draft a tree" guide, is to describe the prompt template so
  thoroughly that it reads like a feature request for an ingestion pipeline. §6.7's
  "explicitly not built" list exists precisely to head this off; keep the document
  describing a **human-run, four-step manual process** with a static template as an
  artifact, not a system.
- **This task is scoped after T21, not before, on purpose.** CONTRIBUTING.md's worked
  examples need to point at real, already-authored, already-valid trees — writing this
  documentation before T05 and T21 land would mean either inventing hypothetical examples
  that could drift from what those tasks actually produced, or leaving the references as
  TODOs. Both are worse than the dependency.
- **F45's checklist question belongs in CONTRIBUTING.md's PR template guidance, not only
  in the rubric.** A contributor decides whether to check a copyleft box before review
  starts, not while reading style guidance — CONTRIBUTING.md's contribution-flow section
  is where they will actually encounter it.
- Cross-reference `docs/PRIOR-ART.md` §7.3 directly in the professionalization section of
  the style rubric — it is the concrete, citable case (MakerSkillTree issue #47, the
  Cooking tree's "teach a cooking class" at the ceiling) that makes the rule land as more
  than an abstract preference.

### Judgment calls worth knowing

- **Step 4 is quoted from the *current* §6.7, not from this doc's transcription of it.**
  T26/F7 lengthened it: the live text runs `lst ids`, `lst validate`, `lst baseline`,
  `lst compile`, `lst lint`, with `lst ids` leading because the draft has no uids and rule 16
  fails a tree that lacks them. Quoting this doc's older two-command version would have
  shipped an authoring guide that omits two gates.
- **The prompt template is versioned inline with a changelog line**, not as a separate file.
  §6.7 says "versioned in the repository so that its output quality is itself reviewable";
  keeping it in the guide means the rules it carries and the prose explaining them cannot
  drift apart.
- **F29's caveat is stated, not implied.** CONTRIBUTING.md's exemplar table says outright
  that modules and track titles are invisible in today's renderer, so a modular tree looks
  linear on screen. Author them for the structure.
- **The `all`/F46 warning is the longest section of the rubric's requirement-group chapter**,
  because the consequence — a dismissal inside an `all` group at or below the blocking level
  capping the skill permanently — is not something an author would derive on their own.
- **No release checklist or accessibility table was written into CONTRIBUTING.md.**
  Per T20's resolution, it links to `docs/RELEASE-CHECKLIST.md` instead.
