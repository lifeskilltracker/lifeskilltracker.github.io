# Candidate Skill Library

**Date:** 2026-08-03
**Purpose:** Stress-test the domain taxonomy against a realistic long-run library, and check whether skills distribute sensibly or whether domains need adding, splitting, or merging.

**Method.** A Haiku agent generated 120 candidates from Wikipedia's Hobby taxonomy, Scouting merit badges, adult-education catalogues, and graded-framework directories. That list was then verified and corrected: near-duplicates merged, meta-entries removed, and gaps filled. The raw list showed a strong sampling bias toward skills with formal certification ladders, which under-represented domains lacking a certifying body (People, Mind, Home). Those were filled in manually. Entries marked † were added during verification.

Sources used by Haiku model:
  - Wikipedia Hobby article and Category:Hobbies
  - Boy Scouts merit badges (2026 list)
  - Girl Scouts Badges & Journeys
  - ABRSM Music Grades
  - CEFR Language Levels
  - Elo Chess Rating System
  - Yosemite Decimal & V-Scale Climbing Systems
  - Martial Arts Belt Rankings
  - PADI Scuba Diving Certifications
  - WSET Wine Certifications
  - Coffee Q-Grader Certification
  - ACF Culinary Certifications
  - FAA Pilot Licensing
  - FCC Amateur Radio Licensing
  - USA Fencing Ratings
  - USAG Gymnastics Levels
  - AWS Certifications Path
  - Master Beekeeping Programs
  - Geocaching Difficulty Ratings
  - FIAP Photography Awards

**Inclusion bar.** Trainable and progressive · has concrete testable milestones · deep enough for ~10 levels · someone would actually track it.

**Total: 164 candidates.**

## Findings

**1. Outdoors & Nature is a genuine domain, not a geometric convenience.** Seventeen candidates with no coherent home under the previous seven. Scuba would have gone to Body, foraging to Home, navigation and birding to Mind — a scatter across three domains with no shared principle, which is the grab-bag symptom that disqualified "Practical" in the original four-domain design. Outdoors is a single-axis arena of capability like the other domains: it is *where* the capability is exercised, and the environment genuinely determines the skill content. It also carries strong graded frameworks (PADI, RYA, BCU star awards, river grades, alpine grades, orienteering courses).

**2. Runaway Making is now confirmed empirically, not just predicted.** At 45 of 164 (27%), Making is 1.7× the next-largest domain and larger than Mind, Play, and Work & Money combined. The earlier research flagged this as the strongest argument against merging Creative and Technical; the distribution bears it out. D20's split trigger should be treated as a near-certainty rather than a contingency, and the split axis should be chosen now even if executed later.

**3. The remaining seven domains are well balanced** — 14 to 27 candidates each, no domain below 9%. No merges are indicated, and no further domain is implied by the data. The set appears stable.

**4. Some skills multiply, which distorts any count.** Languages (one tree per language), instruments, and martial arts are each open-ended families sharing a structure. Six languages appear here; there could plausibly be thirty. This inflates People and Body relative to their true conceptual breadth, and argues for a tree-family or template mechanism so contributors aren't hand-authoring thirty near-identical language trees. Not currently in the schema.

**5. Collecting resists placement.** Wikipedia's hobby taxonomy has Collecting at top level, but collecting skills (coins, stamps, cards) are thin on trainable progression — mostly acquisition plus connoisseurship. Authentication and grading expertise *is* trainable. Recommend a facet tag rather than a domain, and let individual collecting skills sit with their subject matter.

**6. Secondary-domain load is heaviest on Making and Mind**, which are the two domains that most often supply the craft or cognition half of another domain's skill. This validates the primary/secondary design — under a single-domain model, aquascaping, bonsai, dance, and drone piloting would each have lost real information.
